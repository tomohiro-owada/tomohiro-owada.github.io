Pax Silica is a U.S.-led framework for semiconductor and AI supply chains. In the name of reducing dependence on China, it brings in allied countries, and in the Philippines a large industrial hub is moving forward in New Clark City, in Capas, Tarlac.

I think a country that jumps into a framework without settling the details is likely to end up as the side that offers up land and workers. I see a country that cannot wait as a typical case of being used by a stronger power.

There is also talk of 200,000 jobs. But from the outside, it is not possible to see where the money flows.

## The "200,000" after completion

The figure released in July by Joshua Bingcang, president of BCDA (the Bases Conversion and Development Authority), is an estimate of 130,000 to 190,000 direct jobs after the hub is completed. In [reporting](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/2269242/pax-silica-hub-to-bring-p180-billion-in-govt-income-190000-jobs-bcda), it is sometimes rounded to "almost 200,000." The explanation was that 90 percent of those jobs would be for Filipinos. A figure of 500,000 to 800,000 indirect jobs has also been put forward, on the same after-completion premise.

Construction is expected to start in the first quarter of 2028. Even in BCDA's own explanations, the time to completion has a range: in July it was 10 to 15 years, and in August it was also reported as [a plan to develop over 30 years from 2028](https://www.philstar.com/business/2026/08/09/2547911/bcda-sets-30-year-development-plan-pax-silica-ai-hub). Whether the work is only for the construction period, or permanent jobs that remain after completion, no breakdown has been released.

The site is 1,620 hectares. The Philippines joined this framework in April 2026.

## The contractor's name has not been released

As of November 10, the framework agreement for this hub had still [not been signed](https://www.philstar.com/business/2026/11/11/2569233/philippines-us-may-sign-pax-silica-ai-hub-framework-within-2026). DTI (the Department of Trade and Industry) explained that it would not sign at the November ASEAN (Association of Southeast Asian Nations) summit, and was aiming to do so within the year. The parties to the agreement are said [not to be governments, but BCDA and "a U.S. counterpart"](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2026/09/14/bcda-u-s-counterpart-craft-master-deal-on-pax-silica-developments/), yet that counterpart's name has not been made public.

The names of the companies that would move in have also not been released. A [September report](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/regions/1003016/aeta-tribe-in-limbo-over-us-led-pax-silica-tech-hub/story/) wrote that no tenant companies had been confirmed yet. In June, BCDA said more than 50 firms had shown interest, and when asked about the first tenants, it [did not disclose the names](https://business.inquirer.net/595725/50-firms-keen-on-ph-pax-silica-hub).

If it is not clear who will develop the site and who will move in, then from the outside it is also not possible to trace who profits from this plan.

On November 15, there was a protest against this plan in Manila, and [clashes with police and arrests were also reported](https://www.rappler.com/philippines/pax-silica-protest-arrest-asean-opening-manila/). The opposition is to being displaced without adequate consultation, and to water and electricity running short.

## The figures on land also do not line up

Alongside the job promises, the figures on land also do not line up. BCDA says those subject to relocation number [fewer than 50 households](https://business.inquirer.net/616678/relocation-of-settlers-at-us-backed-pax-silica-site-to-begin-in-2027). Meanwhile, a local Aeta (Indigenous) chieftain has said the population of Sapang Kawayan is [about 1,000 people](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/economy/998553/gov-t-won-t-spend-anything-on-pax-silica-hub-development-says-bcda/story/). The way of counting may differ, but from the outside, who is affected does not line up.

Compensation has also come up. The Aeta side has said they were offered 30 pesos per square meter and [refused it](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/regions/1003016/aeta-tribe-in-limbo-over-us-led-pax-silica-tech-hub/story/). In August, BCDA explained that the land belongs to the government, so it is not a matter of buying it, and that [financial assistance would be provided if there is relocation](https://www.philstar.com/business/2026/08/09/2547911/bcda-sets-30-year-development-plan-pax-silica-ai-hub). That unit rate is the same level as the financial assistance shown for New Clark City in 2019. BCDA itself announced at the time that it was [300,000 pesos per hectare, or 30 pesos per square meter](https://bcda.gov.ph/news/new-clark-city-project-hires-more-300-aeta-workers).

In August, Juan Miguel Cuna, then acting secretary of DENR (the Department of Environment and Natural Resources), [told Congress](https://www.abs-cbn.com/news/nation/2026/8/26/no-environmental-impact-assessment-processed-on-pax-silica-denr-1709) that an EIA (environmental impact assessment) for Pax Silica itself had been neither filed nor processed. The reason given was that the substance of the project was still not clear. An ECC (Environmental Compliance Certificate) has been issued for the overall New Clark City development plan, but the explanation is that this is separate from Pax Silica itself.

Only the large employment figures are walking ahead, and the counterpart to the contract, who will leave the land, and the environmental procedures, still cannot be traced from the outside.

## Large job promises have been made before

In the same Clark area, there have been large job promises before. On GGLC (Global Gateway Logistics City), the developer later said that Kuwaiti investors had promised to create at least 200,000 jobs. In 2014 the funding was pulled out, the plan stopped, and [those jobs were not realized](https://punto.com.ph/clark-flagship-project-freezeskuwaitis-pull-out-of-gglc/).

In Subic, the Hanjin shipyard employed up to 30,000 Filipinos at its peak, halted operations in 2019, and the remaining about 3,000 people lost their jobs ([Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/nation/2019/02/17/1894276/hanjin-halts-operations-subic)).

Large employment figures remain only figures until they are completed. The talk of 200,000 jobs is already walking on its own. The name of who will take on the development, and where the money goes, still cannot be seen from the outside. What a country that cannot wait offers up first is land and workers.

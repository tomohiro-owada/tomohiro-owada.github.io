At home I make coffee with Nespresso-style capsules, and what I drink is always an iced Americano. Genuine capsules in the Philippines cost ₱46 a cup, or about 113 yen. Compatible capsules (made by other brands but working in the same machine) are cheaper, so I decided to look for some. While I was at it, I let AI handle everything from the comparison up to just before placing the order.

## Japan or the Philippines?

First, I had my AI that handles exchange rates compare genuine and compatible capsule prices in Japan and the Philippines. The rate on October 23 was ¥1 = ₱0.4044. For compatible capsules, the Philippine prices come from Lazada (a major Southeast Asian online shopping site) and the Japanese prices from Rakuten (one of Japan's biggest online marketplaces).

| Type | Philippines | Japan (in ₱) |
|---|---|---|
| Genuine Original | ₱46 | ¥102.6 (₱41.5) |
| Genuine Vertuo | ₱68 | ¥129.6 (₱52.4) |
| Compatible capsules | ₱17.9–18.5 (Lazada) | ₱16.4 (Rakuten) |
| Starbucks (Nespresso-compatible) | ₱24.8 (sale price) | ₱43.7 |

Genuine capsules are cheaper in Japan: about 10% less for Original and about 20% less for Vertuo (the line with larger, dome-shaped capsules). Compatible capsules, on the other hand, run ₱16–18 each and cost almost the same on Lazada and Rakuten, so there's little point in buying them from Japan. Starbucks capsules were actually far cheaper in the Philippines.

## Picking B Coffee on Lazada

I usually drink 100% Arabica coffee, so I narrowed the compatible capsules available on Lazada down to B Coffee. Apparently, both the product page and the official site said every flavor is 100% Arabica.

The first pick for iced Americano was Colombia (Americano). The 20-pack was on sale, which worked out to about 41 yen a cup, just under 40% of the 113 yen for genuine. I decided to buy 60 at once and had the AI take it all the way to the order confirmation screen.

## The cheapest flavor expired in 3 days

Then, at the confirmation screen, the AI called a time-out. The Colombia option's name literally read "10-27-2026." It was October 24, and delivery was expected on October 28–29. The coffee would be past its best-before date by the time it arrived.

The 40% discount seems to have been a clearance of stock close to expiry. The low price had a reason.

In the end, I went with two 20-packs of Forte (Ristretto), labeled "New Stocks." That was the AI's recommendation too: it's 100% Arabica and the strongest flavor meant for drinking black, so it shouldn't taste washed out in an iced Americano as the ice melts. The total came to ₱1,127 (about 2,780 yen) including shipping, about 69 yen a cup. Pricier than the Colombia, but still well below genuine.

## How I split the work with AI

What I'd decided for this purchase was how much to leave to the AI and where I'd take over.

- AI: comparing products, building the cart, and clicking through up to the order confirmation screen
- Me: checking what's on the confirmation screen and deciding whether to order
- Me: entering the card's security code (CVV)

When Lazada asked for the CVV at payment, one option was to hand over the whole card number and let the AI type it in. I didn't want to hand over my card number, though, so I had the AI pass me just the CVV entry screen as it was. I typed that part in myself and pressed "Pay Now." Afterward, the AI checked the order history to confirm the order had gone through.

Comparing and clicking through screens is tedious, so I delegate it. The final push that moves money, and my card details, stay with me. With that split, asking AI to shop for me never means money moving without my knowing.

The expiry problem was caught at the same point, because the flow always showed me the confirmation screen before the order was finalized. Then again, I was the one who decided on "60 of the Colombia" on the spot. If the AI hadn't noticed, 60 cups' worth of expired coffee would have landed at my door.
